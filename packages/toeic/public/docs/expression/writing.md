  - Write a sentence based on a picture (2')
  - Respond to a written request (10')
  - Write an opinion essay (30')

Title
: Respond to an email

Prompt
: Reply as the event organiser. Suggest two ways to solve the problem and ask one question about the delivery.

  > From: Alex Chen, Northbank Catering  
  > Subject: Friday’s breakfast meeting  
  > 
  > Our delivery van is unavailable on Friday morning, so we cannot deliver your order at 8:00 as planned. We can have it ready for collection at 7:30, or deliver it at 9:15 using another vehicle. Please let us know how you would like to proceed.

Form
:
  :::: tp-textfield {multiline clearable rows="6" label="Respond to an email"}
  ::::

Feedback
: Use this checklist to review your work :

  :::: tp-checkbox-list {label="Self-assessment"}
  - Suggest two workable solutions.
  - Ask a relevant delivery question.
  - Use a clear, polite email structure.
  - Check grammar and vocabulary.
  ::::

Solution
:
  Dear Alex,\
  Thank you for letting me know.\
  One option is for a colleague to collect the order at 7:30.\
  Alternatively, we could serve coffee when guests arrive and move breakfast to 9:30, after your later delivery.\
  Could you confirm whether the food will arrive ready to serve?\
  Once I have checked staff availability, I will confirm our preferred option this afternoon.\
  Best regards,\
  Sam
:::

Title
: Opinion essay

Prompt
: Is it better for a company to train existing employees for new roles or recruit people who already have the required skills? Explain your view with reasons and examples. Aim for at least 300 words; quality and relevance matter as well as length.

Form
: 
  :::: tp-textfield {multiline clearable rows="6" label="Your response — Opinion essay"}
  ::::

Feedback
: 
  :::: tp-checkbox-list {label="Self-assessment"}
  - State and sustain a clear position.
  - Support ideas with specific reasons and examples.
  - Organise paragraphs and connect ideas.
  - Use accurate, varied grammar and vocabulary.
  - Review the response for relevance and completeness.
  ::::

Solution
: Here is an example of an essay. Other positions are valid when supported by clear reasons and relevant examples.

  Possible plan
  :
    1. Position: prioritise internal training, but recruit externally for urgent specialist needs.
    2. Reason: existing staff understand customers and processes; illustrate with a service team adopting new software.
    3. Reason: development opportunities encourage retention; explain the benefit and the investment required.
    4. Counterargument: a new product may require expertise nobody has; external recruitment brings fresh methods.
    5. Conclusion: combine long-term development with targeted recruitment.
      Example response:

  Example
  : 
    A company that needs new skills must decide whether to develop its current workforce or hire people from outside. In my view, training existing employees should usually be the first choice because it builds on their experience and strengthens their commitment to the organisation. However, external recruitment is sometimes necessary, especially when specialist knowledge is needed urgently.

    First, existing employees already understand the company's customers, procedures and working culture. This knowledge makes it easier for them to apply new skills to real problems. For example, imagine a customer service team introducing new software to manage enquiries. Its members know which questions customers ask most often and where delays usually occur. With practical training, they can use the software to improve those processes. New recruits might know the technology well, but they would still need time to understand the customers and the business.

    Second, development opportunities can encourage employees to stay. People are more likely to see a future with an employer that helps them qualify for more demanding roles. This can reduce the disruption caused by frequent departures and preserve valuable experience within the team. Nevertheless, effective training requires investment. Managers must provide suitable courses, time to practise and support from experienced colleagues. They should also explain how employees can use their new skills, rather than offering training without any opportunity for progression.

    There are situations, however, in which recruiting externally is the better option. A company launching a product that uses an unfamiliar technology may need expertise that none of its employees currently possesses. Training someone from the beginning could take too long, particularly if the launch date is close. An experienced recruit can contribute immediately and introduce methods learned elsewhere. Ideally, that person should also share knowledge with existing staff, so the benefits extend beyond a single appointment.

    In conclusion, companies should make internal development their long-term priority while recruiting selectively for urgent or highly specialised needs. Combining these approaches allows a business to retain its people, respond to new challenges and build the skills it will need in the future.
:::
