# Speaking

  Here we outline the different types of speaking tasks in the TOEIC:

  - Read a text aloud (2')
  - Describe a picture (2')
  - Respond to questions (1')
  - Respond to questions using information provided (2')
  - Express an opinion (5')


Prepare your answer, then use the recorder to practise speaking. Start and stop recording yourself. Download each recording before leaving this page. Microphone access is optional: you can also practise aloud without recording.

::: tp-fill-blank-question {open}
Title
: Read aloud

Prompt
: Prepare the text below, then use the recorder to read it aloud clearly (start and stop recording yourself).

  > Welcome to the Riverside Business Centre.\
  > Visitors attending today’s design seminar should collect a badge at reception before going to the second floor.\
  > Refreshments will be available during the morning break.\
  > If you need assistance connecting your laptop, a member of our technical team will be happy to help.

  :::: tp-audio-recorder {transcript="#read_aloud"}
  ::::

Form
:
  :::: tp-textfield {multiline id="read_aloud"}
  ::::

Feedback
: Use this checklist to review your work :

  :::: tp-checkbox-list {label="Self-assessment"}
  - Words are intelligible.
  - Stress and intonation support the meaning.
  - Phrasing is fluent without rushing.
  ::::

Solution
: Read all sentences, grouping words naturally. Pause after “Centre” and at sentence endings. Give clear stress to “badge”, “second floor” and “technical team”.

  :::: tp-text-to-speech {show-text lang="en-GB" voice=Daniel pause-between-lines="500"}
  ::::: script {type="tp/txt"}
    Welcome to the Riverside Business Centre. 
    Visitors attending today’s design seminar should collect a badge at reception before going to the second floor. 
    Refreshments will be available during the morning break. 
    If you need assistance connecting your laptop, a member of our technical team will be happy to help.
  :::::
:::

Title
: Express an opinion

Prompt
: Should employers let staff choose their starting time? State your view and support it with reasons or examples.

  :::: tp-audio-recorder {transcript="#express_an_opinion"}
  ::::

Form
: 
  :::: tp-textfield {multiline id="express_an_opinion"}
  ::::

Feedback
: 
  :::: tp-checkbox-list {label="Self-assessment"}
  - State a clear position.
  - Develop reasons and examples.
  - Use coherent structure and varied language.
  ::::

Solution
:
  :::: tp-text-to-speech  {lang="en-GB" show-text}
  ::::: script {type="tp/txt"}
    I think flexible starting times are helpful when teams agree on shared working hours. Staff can avoid busy trains or manage family responsibilities, which may improve concentration. In my previous team, everyone was available from ten until three, so arranging meetings was still easy. However, customer-facing roles may need a fixed rota. I would therefore offer flexibility where the job allows it, while ensuring that customers and colleagues can still get support.
  :::::
  ::::
